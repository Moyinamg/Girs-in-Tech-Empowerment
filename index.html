Grace Hopper

1906 to 2002 · US Navy Rear Admiral

Grace invented the first compiler, a program that translated human-readable commands into machine code. Before her, only experts who understood raw binary could program computers.

She also popularised the term "debugging" after her team found a literal moth inside a computer causing errors.

She received the Presidential Medal of Freedom in 2016 for a lifetime of transforming computing.

Katherine Johnson

1918 to 2020 · NASA Mathematician

Katherine calculated the orbital trajectories that put the first Americans in space and on the Moon. Astronaut John Glenn refused to fly unless Katherine personally verified the computer calculations.

As a Black woman in the 1950s and 60s, she worked at NASA under segregation, proving her brilliance against every barrier.

Her story was told in the 2016 film Hidden Figures. She received the Presidential Medal of Freedom in 2015.

Ada Lovelace

1815 to 1852 · English Mathematician

Ada wrote the world's first algorithm designed to be processed by a computing machine, nearly 100 years before computers were built. She collaborated with inventor Charles Babbage on his Analytical Engine.

She was also the daughter of the famous poet Lord Byron. She recognised that machines could go beyond calculation and be used for music, graphics, and more.

Ada Lovelace Day is now celebrated globally every October to honour women in STEM.
